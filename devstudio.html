DevStudio and GamePulse

Desktop app, then web app · an AI game studio

A virtual game studio staffed by 25 specialist AI agents. DevStudio was the desktop version, running on a local AI model. GamePulse was the same idea rebuilt for the web, with accounts and streaming replies.

The studio

Twenty-five roles in five groups, all sharing one AI model and told apart by their briefs.

Core team.Project manager, game designer, programmer, analyst, marketing, UI/UX.
Art and audio.Sound designer, character artist, environment artist, concept artist, animator.
Writing.Narrative designer, dialogue writer, level designer, puzzle designer.
Business.Monetisation strategist, growth, publisher relations, QA tester.
Specialists.Player psychologist, localisation, accessibility, viral content, AI systems, games lawyer.

How you worked with it

Studios.Rooms where two or more agents discuss a task together. Mention one by name to direct a message at them.
Offices.One-to-one rooms with a single specialist.
Briefs.Save an important stretch of conversation, such as a design decision, and pull it into any later chat by name.

GamePulse, the web version

The same studio as a website: sign in with email, Google or GitHub, create projects and rooms, and watch replies stream in as they're written. It ran as a free open beta.

Why both are shelved

Neither produced work I would publish. Twenty-five agents talking to each other gave long, agreeable conversations and no finished artefact. Fixing that meant rebuilding the core, with a router and real tools, and there was no path to it paying for itself.

What I took from it: Haakim has four assistants, not twenty-five. Each has one job, a short brief, and a list of things it must never do.
