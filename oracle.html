O.R.A.C.L.E

Telegram bot · local AI · memory and scheduler

A personal assistant I talk to through Telegram. It runs entirely on my own PC with a local AI model, remembers what I tell it, runs jobs on a schedule, and researches Reddit, RSS feeds and the web for me.

What it does

Remembers.Two kinds of memory: a small profile of my preferences, and a store of notes and facts it can search by meaning, not just by matching words.
Runs on a schedule.One-off reminders and repeating jobs, kept through restarts and set to India time.
Researches.Checks subreddits, RSS feeds and web pages, then saves what it finds to memory.
Drafts in my voice.Reads my brand notes before writing anything public, so drafts sound like me.
Two speeds.A small fast model answers everyday chat. A larger, slower one handles deep questions and the scheduled jobs.
Guided or fast.A bare command opens buttons that walk me through it. The same command with details skips straight to the result.

Design decisions

Silent to strangers.Only my own account is allowed. Anyone else gets no reply at all, so the bot doesn't even reveal that it exists.
Everything stays local.The AI model, the memory and the schedule all live on my PC. Nothing is sent to an AI company.
Safe web reading.The web reader refuses addresses that point back into my own network, and re-checks after every redirect.

What it taught me

A silent failure is worse than a loud one. I changed the chat model once, and memory search quietly stopped working, because notes saved with one model can't be compared with another. Nothing crashed; the answers just got worse. Now every saved note is labelled with the model that made it, and search ignores the ones that don't match.

It became the first draft of Haakim. The scheduled night jobs, the two-part memory and the rule about staying private all carried over.
