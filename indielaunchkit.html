IndieLaunchKit

Desktop app · itch.io publish-assist

A desktop app for indie developers: paste your itch.io link, answer a short intake, and get a store page that's ready to publish, with every field filled, an asset checklist, a layout and a score.

Why I started it

I shipped my own game, The Wasted Times, on itch.io, and by my own scoring the launch page was a 5 or 6 out of 10. Indie developers are makers, not marketers: we skip the launch chores or do them badly. Steam has plenty of tools for this. itch.io has almost none.

So I was the first customer. The tool exists to make my next launch a 10 out of 10, and my own shipped game became its test case.

How it works

One run takes a game from a few answers to a scored page.

Intake. Twenty questions about the game. This is the main input and it's required.
Optional documents. A design document or pitch can be dropped in. The app reads it and pre-fills the intake, which the developer then corrects.
Writing. Six separate prompts each write one part of the page: the short description, the long description, tags and so on.
Checking. Every answer is checked against a schema and retried up to three times if it's malformed.
Scoring. The assembled page is scored against my rubric: 15 items worth 85 points, covering what a strong itch.io page needs.

Design decisions

Do one job.I narrowed the scope twice in one day. Competitor research turned up a direct rival for the marketing half, so version one became publish-assist only.
The AI lives inside the app.A 4.4 GB language model ships in the installer, with a smaller one for low-memory PCs. Nothing to install, no account, and no server bill for me.
The title is never written by AI.A game's name belongs to its maker. The app copies it exactly as typed and marks which fields came from the developer and which were generated.
The sharing test.Every feature had to pass one question: would a stranger share the output with a friend within ten seconds? If not, it wasn't built.
Answers first, files second.The intake is the source of truth. Documents only enrich it, and the app never reads the game's own files.
Test without the model.A separate checker replays saved outputs through the rubric, so quality can be tested in seconds without running the AI.

Why I shelved it

Shelved before release, for four reasons. The generator, the document reader and the rubric scorer all work end to end on my own game. I stopped anyway.

I didn't have enough experience myself. I had shipped one game on itch.io. That's not enough to tell other developers how to launch theirs.

A general AI assistant can already do this. Most of what the app produced, someone can now get by asking an assistant like Claude directly.

The market is small. Few of the people shipping on itch.io would pay for this service.

Steam was no way out. Growing meant covering Steam, where I haven't shipped a game. People who have know far more and can teach it far better than I could.
